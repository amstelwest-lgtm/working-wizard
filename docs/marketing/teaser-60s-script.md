# MILŌN — 60-second teaser video script

Two cuts live in this file.

- **Cut A** is the first draft: pain-first, mechanism-second, language lifted from the landing page.
  Keep it as a reference, not as the film to produce.
- **Cut B** is the one to produce. It keeps the same 60-second spine and the same animation grammar,
  but it sells the two dream outcomes instead of the three product features.

Voice-over in both cuts runs ~150 words, which sits in 60 seconds at a calm, unhurried South African
read. Do not speed the read up to fit more copy in — the whole point of the brand is that it lowers
your heart rate.

---

## Why Cut A was written the way it was

This is the honest version of the first-draft rationale, including where it was wrong.

### What the first draft was trying to do

A 60-second teaser for a brand nobody has heard of has one job: make a cold viewer feel a specific
problem, glimpse a new possibility, and remember one sentence. It is not a product tour, a feature
list, or a sales deck. The first draft treated that constraint seriously and made four bets:

1. **Open on a diagnosis, not a product.** "Most businesses don't fail. They drift." is already the
   strongest line on the landing page. It names a condition the viewer already lives in, which is
   why it works as a hook.
2. **Hit both buyers, because Milōn is a two-sided product.** A 60-second film that only sells to
   owners leaves the accountant cold, and the other way around. The first draft split the middle
   ~28 seconds into owner pain, then accountant pain, then used "the bridge" as the one idea that
   holds both audiences in the same frame.
3. **Show the mechanism, not a slogan.** Health score, 13-week forecast, ranked fixes, notes on the
   number — those are the things the product actually does today, and they map onto pictures an
   animator can draw.
4. **Stay inside claims the product can stand behind.** `/for-accountants` already refuses to quote
   uplift that has not been measured. The first draft inherited that discipline and left "R1 200+
   per client" off the table on purpose.

Those four bets are still right for a teaser. The mistake was what got poured into the middle.

### What it borrowed from sales and teaser craft

Not a single named guru was sitting on the desk, but the structure is not invented. It is a
compressed version of a few well-worn rules:

| Rule | Where it shows up in Cut A |
| --- | --- |
| **PAS — problem, agitate, solve** (classic direct response, Schwartz) | Drift → year-end / cash crisis → the bridge |
| **The first five seconds do most of the work** (Ogilvy, every media buyer) | The gold line losing its nerve, then "They drift." |
| **Customer is the hero, product is the guide** (StoryBrand / Miller) | Attempted. Undercut in section 4 when the line becomes "Milōn is the bridge" and the product takes the lead |
| **Don't sell the drill, sell the hole** (Levitt) | Broken. Section 4 sells the drill: score, forecast, ranked list |
| **Dream Outcome × Likelihood / Time × Effort** (Hormozi's value equation) | Broken. Cut A sold likelihood ("here is the mechanism") and ignored the dream outcome on both sides |
| **People buy the person they become** (identity-based selling) | Broken for the accountant. "Can't do it for eighty clients" is a capacity complaint. "This is how the practice becomes an advisory firm" is an identity sale |
| **Jobs to be done** (Christensen) | Glimpsed, not named. The owner's actual job is see / diagnose / look ahead / act. Cut A named the tools instead of the job |
| **A teaser withholds; an explainer dumps** | Cut A is a short explainer wearing a teaser's clothes |
| **One idea per film** (Apple, most good 60-second work) | The one idea was "the bridge." That is the category idea. It is not either buyer's dream |

So: the skeleton is standard 60-second craft. The flesh was landing-page copy. That is why it feels
careful and a bit small.

### Why the first draft did not say "CFO / finance department in your pocket"

Three reasons, only one of which still holds.

**1. The repo already had a quieter sentence, and the draft copied it.**
The landing page, `/for-owners`, and the recent "lead with the bridge" positioning all describe
Milōn as a financial health platform and a shared workspace. "CFO in your pocket" and "a whole
finance department" are not how the product currently talks about itself. A first draft written
from the codebase will inherit the house voice. That is a research habit, not a sales decision.

**2. Claims anxiety.**
A health score, a cash forecast and a ranked playbook are not a CFO. A CFO sits in the meeting,
argues with the bank, and owns the decision. Over-claiming that in a cold teaser is how you get a
viewer who signs up, does not feel a CFO, and leaves. That caution is real. It is also the wrong
caution for a teaser. A teaser sells the *feeling of the job being done* — constant overview,
diagnosis, a view of the future, an action plan that falls out of the numbers. The product page
can then be honest about what is software and what is still the accountant. Cut A did the product
page's job in the teaser's slot.

**3. Dual-audience maths.**
In 60 seconds you get one transformation, maybe two if they share a spine. The first draft spent
the owner seconds on *pain* (SARS document, cash crisis) because pain is faster to film than a
four-part operating loop. That was a trade. It was the wrong trade. The four-part loop — overview,
diagnosis, forward look, action plan — *is* the concept, and it films cleanly as four orbiting
bodies around the owner, which is already the visual language of the brand.

The user's frame is the stronger one. It is also more precise than "CFO." "CFO in your pocket" is
the category shortcut (people already know what that should feel like). The four jobs underneath
it are what stop the line from being empty: you are not promising a person, you are promising the
work a finance function does — watch the business, name the problem, look ahead, tell you what to
do. That is exactly what the product computes.

### Why the first draft did not sell accountants a plug-and-play path to advisory

Same pattern. The first draft sold the *constraint* (you cannot advise eighty clients by hand) and
left the *identity* on the table (this is how the practice becomes an advisory firm).

That happened because `/for-accountants` is deliberately understated — "we are not going to quote
you a time saving we have not measured" — and because "AI-powered portal" is the most abused phrase
in current B2B video. The draft avoided both, and in doing so avoided the actual sale.

For a practice principal, the buying emotion is not "I wish I had a live health radar." It is:

- I have been telling myself for five years that we should move from compliance to advisory.
- I do not have a product, a portal, or a way to onboard the book without another implementation
  project.
- I can see AI coming for the low-margin work and I do not have a safe way to bring it in without
  looking reckless to clients.
- I need a channel where the monthly conversation about the numbers actually lives, instead of
  living in a year-end meeting and a buried email.

"Plug-and-play" is doing real work in that list. It attacks the effort side of the value equation —
the thing that kills advisory transformations is not desire, it is the rebuild. "Modern AI-powered
portal" is the artefact they can show a client. "The channel" is the relationship change. "The way
we incorporate AI" is the identity change for the firm. Cut A got to none of those because it
stopped at the pain that makes them necessary.

The caution that still holds: do not put a rand-per-client uplift on screen, and do not say the
platform *is* the advisor. The judgement stays with the accountant. The film can still say, without
lying, that this is how a compliance practice starts selling advice every month.

### What to keep from Cut A

- The hook. Do not touch "They drift."
- The gold line as the one continuous graphic from first frame to last.
- Dual audience in one film, with the bridge as the hinge — provided the two sides are now dream
  outcomes, not feature lists.
- Claims discipline on numbers we have not measured.
- Casting, palette, motion grammar, mute-first captions.

### What Cut B changes

Owner seconds sell the finance function in your pocket, named as four jobs: overview, diagnosis,
forward look, action plan. Accountant seconds sell the identity shift: plug-and-play advisory,
clients on an AI-powered portal, a real channel, a way to bring AI into the firm. The concept line
is no longer "score, forecast, list." It is "that channel, both sides, every month."

## Cut A — pain-first (first draft, do not produce)

### Shot list


### 1 · Hook — 0:00–0:06

**Visual.** Deep near-black field. A single thin gold line draws itself left to right like a revenue
chart, confident and rising. Halfway across it loses its nerve: it wobbles, flattens, and starts
sliding down and off the bottom of the frame. The camera does not move. We just watch it go.

**VO.** "Most businesses don't fail. They drift."

**On screen.** `They drift.` — set in the serif, gold, fading up under the line as it sinks.

**Audio.** Low sub-bass drone. One soft, hollow tone on the word "drift."

---

### 2 · The owner's pain — 0:06–0:20

**Visual.** The falling line becomes the desk edge of an owner working late — a simple line-art
figure, gold on black, lit by one lamp. Papers, invoices and a laptop slowly orbit their head like
planets: they are the centre of a system they cannot read. On "months later," an annual financial
statement thuds onto the desk and a calendar behind them flips fast through a whole year. On "the
morning it arrives," the lamp light snaps from gold to a cold red and the orbit stops dead.

**VO.** "You built something real. But you find out how it's actually going months later, in a
document written for SARS. And cash trouble only announces itself the morning it arrives."

**On screen.** `Annual financial statements` stamped on the document, then a red chip: `Cash short —
today`.

**Audio.** Paper, a clock ticking up in speed, then a hard low hit on the red snap. Then silence.

---

### 3 · The accountant's pain — 0:20–0:34

**Visual.** Pull back and to the right. The owner's desk becomes one lit window in a grid of many
windows — an accountant's client book. Our accountant sits at the centre. They reach toward one
window and it lights gold; the moment they let go it dims again, because they only have two hands
and eighty clients. On "year-end," the whole grid dims to grey except a handful of windows that
someone had time for.

**VO.** "Your accountant already knows. They just can't do it for eighty clients at once — so the
advice arrives at year-end, when it's history instead of help."

**On screen.** A quiet counter in the corner ticking `01 / 80` upward and stalling around `09 / 80`.

**Audio.** A single repeated piano note that never resolves. Room tone thins out.

---

### 4 · The concept — 0:34–0:48

**Visual.** The hero move of the film. Two constellations sit on opposite sides of frame — the
owner's small orbit on the left, the accountant's grid of client stars on the right, separated by
dark empty space. On the word "bridge," a gold line strikes across the gap and connects them, and
the MILŌN mark forms at the midpoint. Light travels both ways along the line. The two sides resolve
into one shared workspace: a gold gauge counting up to a single score, a 13-week cash curve drawing
across with a visible dip at week 6 that pulses once, and three ranked fixes stacking in underneath.

**VO.** "Milōn is the bridge. One shared workspace, where your figures become a single health score,
a thirteen-week cash forecast, and a ranked list of what to fix first."

**On screen.** `78 · Health score` on the gauge · `Cash dip — week 6` on the curve · `Your next
three moves` over the stacked list.

**Audio.** The drone lifts into warm strings. One clean chime lands exactly on the score number.

---

### 5 · The payoff — 0:48–0:57

**Visual.** Close on one figure in the forecast. An accountant's note glides in and docks directly
onto that number — not floating beside it, attached to it. Cut wide: the same screen, mirrored, one
half on a laptop and one half on a phone, both showing the identical view. On "every month," a
calendar behind them ticks over twelve times, and the score nudges upward each time.

**VO.** "Your accountant's notes land on the exact number they're about. Every month. Both sides.
The same screen."

**On screen.** `+9 pts · this quarter`

**Audio.** Strings resolve. Soft click as the note docks.

---

### 6 · Logo — 0:57–1:00

**Visual.** Everything falls away to black. The MILŌN mark draws itself in gold — the circle, then
the four-point line inside it. The tagline types on beneath. Hold, then a slow fade.

**VO.** "Milōn. Know your numbers. Sleep at night."

**On screen.**

```
MILŌN
Know your numbers. Sleep at night.
milon.co.za
```

**Audio.** One last chime, then the drone drops out on the cut to black.

## Animation direction

**Style.** 2D vector motion design, flat with light glow. Thin gold line-art on near-black, in the
register of an orrery or a star chart rather than a corporate explainer. Characters are simple
line-art silhouettes with no faces — this keeps them universal and keeps the film cheap to produce
and easy to re-cut later.

**Palette.** Straight from the landing page.

| Role         | Hex       |
| ------------ | --------- |
| Background   | `#050507` |
| Gold         | `#d4af37` |
| Bright gold  | `#fdee79` |
| Deep gold    | `#ac8400` |
| Cream ink    | `#f2ecdc` |
| Dim ink      | `#9b958a` |
| Risk red     | `#e25c5c` |

Red appears exactly once, in section 2. That is what makes it land.

**Motion grammar.** Everything is drawn, orbited, or connected — nothing slides in from off-screen
like a slide deck. Sections 1 to 3 are cold, slow and slightly claustrophobic; section 4 is the
release, and every transition after it moves outward and upward. Ease everything; no linear moves.

**Transitions.** Use the gold line as the connective tissue between all six sections. The chart line
in section 1 becomes the desk in section 2, the desk becomes a window in the grid in section 3, and
the grid's edge becomes the bridge in section 4. One continuous piece of gold from first frame to
last.

**Typography.** The landing page's serif for the emotive lines ("They drift"), the sans for anything
that is data or UI. Never more than six words on screen at once.

---

## Sound

Sparse. A low sub-bass drone underneath the two pain sections doing most of the emotional work,
warm strings entering only at the bridge in section 4. Three sound events matter and should be the
only things that punch: the hollow tone on "drift," the low hit on the red cash chip, and the clean
chime on the score reveal. Everything else stays under.

Cut a captioned version — most of this will be watched on mute in a feed, so the on-screen text has
to carry the whole argument on its own.

---

## Claims discipline

Keep the teaser to claims the product actually stands behind today.

- **Safe to say.** One health score. Thirty-one ratios. Four pillars. A 13-week cash forecast.
  Over nine hundred ranked fixes. Notes attached to the figure they refer to. Built for South
  African SMEs.
- **Leave out.** The "R1 200+ advisory uplift per client per month" figure and any per-client time
  saving. `/for-accountants` deliberately refuses to quote uplift we have not measured across real
  practices, and a teaser is the worst place to break that discipline.
- **Careful with.** "Eighty clients" in section 3 is a texture number, not a statistic — it reads as
  an example rather than a claim, which is why it works. Do not put it on screen as a stat.

## Cutdowns from the same assets

**30 seconds.** Sections 1, 4 and 6, with one line lifted from each pain section: "Owners find out
months later. Accountants can't do it for eighty clients at once." The bridge does all the work.

**6-second bumper.** The gold line strikes across the gap, the mark forms, the tagline types on.
No voice-over, captions only.

**Two persona edits.** Same film, re-voiced. The owner cut drops section 3 and spends the time on
the cash dip; the accountant cut drops section 2 and spends it on the client grid lighting up all at
once. Useful for splitting paid spend by audience without paying for a second animation.

## Cut B — the one to produce

Same six-beat clock, same gold-line grammar, same palette and sound rules as Cut A. Different
promise in the middle. Owner seconds sell the finance function. Accountant seconds sell the
advisory firm. The bridge is what makes both promises true at once.

### Shot list

### 1 · Hook — 0:00–0:06

**Visual.** Unchanged from Cut A. Deep near-black. A thin gold revenue line draws itself, loses its
nerve, and slides off the bottom of the frame. Camera does not move.

**VO.** "Most businesses don't fail. They drift."

**On screen.** `They drift.`

**Audio.** Low drone. Hollow tone on "drift."

---

### 2 · The owner — a finance department in your pocket — 0:06–0:22

**Visual.** The falling line becomes an orbit around a simple owner silhouette. Four gold bodies
ignite one after another, each labelled as it lights:

1. a live gauge — **overview**
2. a cracked pillar highlighting the weak organ — **diagnosis**
3. the 13-week cash curve drawing forward, dip visible at week 6 — **the next thirteen weeks**
4. three ranked moves stacking out of the dip — **the plan**

On the last beat the four bodies tighten into a small constellation that settles in the owner's
hand, phone-sized. Not a person standing next to them. The work of a finance function, in their
pocket.

**VO.** "What an owner needs is a finance department in their pocket. A constant overview of the
business. A diagnosis of what's actually wrong. An eye on the next thirteen weeks. And an action
plan that comes straight out of those numbers — not out of a year-end meeting."

**On screen.** Four chips, one per beat: `Overview` · `Diagnosis` · `13 weeks ahead` · `The plan`

**Audio.** Each body lights with a soft tick. Warmth comes in under the last line.

---

### 3 · The accountant — plug-and-play advisory — 0:22–0:38

**Visual.** Pull back. The owner's constellation is one lit window in a dark grid — the client
book. The accountant does not tap windows one by one this time. A single gold spine runs the length
of the grid and every window comes up together: each client already sitting inside a small portal,
the firm's mark on the glass. A client walks into one of those windows on their phone. A note
leaves the accountant and docks on a number inside the owner's view. On "incorporate AI," a quiet
draft appears beside a figure, waiting; the accountant's hand signs it before it moves.

**VO.** "What an accountant needs is a plug-and-play way to turn the practice into an advisory firm.
Onboard every client onto a modern, AI-powered portal — and finally have a real channel for talking
about the numbers. That's how a firm brings AI in, without looking reckless."

**On screen.** `Compliance → Advisory` on the spine · `Your portal. Your name.` on a window ·
`Draft. You sign.` on the note.

**Audio.** The unresolved piano note from Cut A's accountant beat now resolves as the grid lights.

---

### 4 · The concept — 0:38–0:50

**Visual.** The hero move, kept. Two constellations, a gold line striking the gap, the MILŌN mark
forming at the midpoint. Light travels both ways. This time we do not linger on a score and a
feature stack — we linger on the traffic on the line: overview, diagnosis, forecast and plan going
one way; a signed note coming back the other.

**VO.** "Milōn is that channel. One shared workspace, both sides. The overview, the diagnosis, the
future, and the plan — every month, under the firm's name."

**On screen.** `One channel. Both sides.`

**Audio.** Strings. The chime now lands on the mark, not on a score number.

**Visual.** Same screen, split: laptop on the accountant's side, phone on the owner's. Identical
view. The calendar behind them ticks twelve months and the owner's constellation holds its shape

**VO.** "Not a year-end event. A standing finance function — and a practice that can finally charge
for it."

**On screen.** `Every month.`

**Audio.** Soft click as the twelfth month lands.

### What Cut B is allowed to say, and what it is not

Say these, they are the sale:

- A finance department in your pocket — meaning the work, not a hired CFO.
- Constant overview, diagnosis, thirteen weeks ahead, action plan from the numbers.
- Plug-and-play path from compliance to advisory.
- Clients onboarded onto a portal that carries the firm's name.
- A channel for talking about the numbers, both sides, every month.
- AI drafts, the accountant signs. That is how the firm incorporates AI without looking reckless.

Do not say these, they are still the traps from Cut A:

- Any rand-per-client uplift, time-saved-per-report, or "replaces your accountant."
- That the platform *is* the CFO, or *is* the advisor. It is the function and the channel. The
  judgement stays human.
- A feature parade (31 ratios, 930 fixes, 10 report formats) — those belong on the site after the
  click.
